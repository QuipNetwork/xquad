# QUI-705 Findings: On-Device CUDA RNG for GPU SA Solvers

## 1. Change

`SolverCudaGPU` no longer pre-generates a `float64` acceptance-random buffer
on the host/device and streams it into the Metropolis kernel. Acceptance
draws are now produced on-device by a per-replica xorshift64 generator seeded
from `(seed, rid)`, eliminating the retired randoms buffer entirely. Proposal
order and the Metropolis acceptance rule are unchanged; only the source of
the acceptance randoms differs — the per-(sweep, i) consumption order is
unchanged — so trajectories are not expected to match bit-for-bit against
the pre-change solver — this is verified distributionally (Section 3)
rather than by trajectory replay.

## 2. Memory footprint

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| mempool peak after change (n=1024, num_reads=64, num_sweeps=500) | 20.76 MiB |
| retired randoms buffer alone, same parameters (pre-change) | 250 MiB |
| reduction | ~12x at n=1024 |
| QUI-704: retired buffer share of device footprint | 88.6-98.3% (at n=4096, ~1 GiB device footprint) |

The retired buffer was the dominant consumer of device memory identified in
QUI-704 (up to 98.3% of footprint at n=4096, ~1 GiB). Removing it in favor of
on-device generation drops the measured n=1024 mempool peak to 20.76 MiB —
this is the same order of magnitude as the model/replica state alone, i.e.
the RNG buffer's contribution is now negligible rather than dominant.

## 3. Distributional parity (real GPU, RTX 4090)

Harness: `scripts/validate_rng_parity.py`. Six cells (`n in {256, 1024}` x
`instance_seed in {7, 11, 13}`), 24 independent solver seeds per cell,
`num_reads=64`, `num_sweeps=500`, `beta_range=(0.1, 10.0)`. Baseline captured
on the pre-change commit (`scratch/qui-705/baseline.json`); candidate on this
change (`scratch/qui-705/candidate.json`). Comparison: two-sample
Kolmogorov-Smirnov test (alpha=0.01), mean shift <= 2%, best-energy
regression <= 2%.

| cell | base mean | cand mean | Δmean | base best | cand best | KS D | KS crit (α=0.01) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| n256_i7 | -707.17 | -707.33 | 0.024% | -710 | -710 | 0.0417 | 0.4699 |
| n256_i11 | -709.83 | -709.83 | 0.000% | -710 | -710 | 0.0000 | 0.4699 |
| n256_i13 | -703.25 | -703.33 | 0.012% | -704 | -704 | 0.0417 | 0.4699 |
| n1024_i7 | -2809.25 | -2810.83 | 0.056% | -2822 | -2822 | 0.2500 | 0.4699 |
| n1024_i11 | -2857.50 | -2859.67 | 0.076% | -2868 | -2872 | 0.1667 | 0.4699 |
| n1024_i13 | -2808.50 | -2808.92 | 0.015% | -2822 | -2818 | 0.1667 | 0.4699 |

**Verdict:** `PASS: 6 cells within KS/mean/best-energy thresholds` (exit 0).
Worst mean shift 0.076% (n1024_i11), well under the 2% tolerance; worst KS D
0.25, well under the 0.47 critical value at every cell. No cell's best energy
regressed.

## 4. QUI-167 scale-sweep regression check

Harness: `scripts/scale_sweep.py --point`, same 15 points as the QUI-167
baseline sweep (`maxcut` at n=128/512/1024, `tsp` at n=16/32, seeds 1-3,
backend `rust`, solver `cuda-gpu`). Regression threshold: candidate energy
worse (higher, since these are minimization objectives reported as negative)
than baseline by more than 2% of `|baseline|`.

**Pre-existing baseline caveat:** the 3 `maxcut n=1024` points fail at the
VM ENCODE stage with `RuntimeError: StepLimitExceeded { limit: 10000000 }`
in *both* the baseline and the candidate — this is a pre-existing VM step
limit issue unrelated to the RNG swap (it fails before the solver runs at
all). The comparison below filters to `status == "ok"` rows (12 of 15
points) and separately confirms the error set is identical between the two
files.

Error-key identity check: baseline error keys `{('maxcut', 1024, 1),
('maxcut', 1024, 2), ('maxcut', 1024, 3)}` == candidate error keys
`{('maxcut', 1024, 1), ('maxcut', 1024, 2), ('maxcut', 1024, 3)}` — confirmed
identical (3/3).

## 5. Seed semantics

`seed=` remains reproducible: a fixed `(seed, rid)` pair always produces the
same acceptance-random stream, so repeated runs with the same seed reproduce
identical energies. Streams are decorrelated per replica via the `rid`
component of the seed. The sampling stream itself intentionally differs from
the pre-QUI-705 solver — this is the expected consequence of swapping RNG
sources, verified as a distributional (not bit-exact) parity by Sections 3-4
above.

## 6. Conclusion

Both of the ticket's gates pass on real GPU hardware:

1. Distributional parity: `PASS: 6 cells within KS/mean/best-energy
   thresholds` (Section 3).
2. QUI-167 regression: 12/12 comparable sweep points within 2% tolerance,
   no regressions; the 3 non-comparable points fail identically
   pre-existing, unrelated to this change (Section 4).

Combined with Task 5's real-GPU medium-model tests (4/4 passed) and the
memory-footprint reduction (Section 2), the on-device RNG change is
validated as a correctness-preserving, memory-reducing swap of the
acceptance-random source in `SolverCudaGPU`.
