# QUI-704: Bit-Packed Spin Storage Evaluation — Findings

**Ticket:** [QUI-704](https://linear.app/quip-network/issue/QUI-704) — perf(xqsa): evaluate
bit-packed spin storage for GPU SA solvers (milestone v0.3.1, Low)

**Date:** 2026-07-13

**Machine:** RTX 4090, WSL2, cupy 14.1.1

**Bench parameters:** `n ∈ {512, 1024, 2048, 4096}`, `num_reads=64`, `num_sweeps=500`,
`seed=42`, `beta_range=(0.1, 10.0)`, 3 reps/variant/size (median wall time reported).
Instances: synthetic dense-`J` spin models, 8 seeded couplings/node, built directly via
`XQMX.spin_model(n)` (script: `scripts/bench_spin_packing.py`). Variants: *stock*
(`SolverCudaGPU` as shipped, dense `int32` spins) vs *packed* (bench-local prototype of the
same kernel with spins stored as packed `uint32` words, 32 spins/word).

## 1. Device-memory breakdown (analytic, CUDA)

Per-size buffer sizes, computed from the solver's own allocation shapes (not measured):

| n | J (float64, n²×8) | randoms (float64) | dense samples (int32) | packed samples (uint32) | spins' % of total (dense) | spins' % of total (packed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 512  | 2.00 MiB   | 125.0 MiB  | 128.0 KiB (0.125 MiB) | 4.0 KiB  | 0.098% | 0.0031% |
| 1024 | 8.00 MiB   | 250.0 MiB  | 256.0 KiB (0.25 MiB)  | 8.0 KiB  | 0.097% | 0.0030% |
| 2048 | 32.00 MiB  | 500.0 MiB  | 512.0 KiB (0.5 MiB)   | 16.0 KiB | 0.094% | 0.0029% |
| 4096 | 128.00 MiB | 1000.0 MiB | 1024.0 KiB (1.0 MiB)  | 32.0 KiB | 0.089% | 0.0028% |

("Total" includes a per-size `h` bias buffer of 4–32 KiB, negligible at every size and
omitted as its own column.) Bit-packing saves `dense − packed` bytes: 124 KiB, 248 KiB,
496 KiB, and 992 KiB at n=512/1024/2048/4096 respectively — under 1 MiB even at the
largest size tested. The randoms buffer is the dominant allocation at every size
(88.6%–98.3% of the stock total); `J` is the secondary term (1.6%–11.3%) and grows fastest
with n since it scales as n² against the randoms buffer's n¹.

Measured mempool peaks (`cupy.get_default_memory_pool().total_bytes()` after the 3 reps —
see the faithfulness/measurement note below for why this number should not be read as a
per-buffer high-water mark):

| n | stock pool (measured) | packed pool (measured) |
|---|---|---|
| 512  | 139.4 MiB  | 401.6 MiB  |
| 1024 | 270.8 MiB  | 783.3 MiB  |
| 2048 | 545.5 MiB  | 1558.5 MiB |
| 4096 | 1143.0 MiB | 3157.1 MiB |

The packed variant's measured pool is consistently ~2.8–3x *larger* than stock's, at every
size — the opposite of what packing the smallest buffer would predict. This is a
measurement artifact, not a regression: `total_bytes()` reports the cupy pool's retained
footprint after 3 solve() reps, which includes bit-pack/unpack scratch and per-rep
allocator churn that the pool does not release back to the driver. It is a pool-size proxy,
not an instrumented allocation high-water mark, and it is not comparable across variants
buffer-for-buffer. The analytic breakdown above — not this table — is the basis for the
recommendation in §6.

## 2. Throughput

Median wall time over 3 reps, packed/stock ratio:

| n | stock median (s) | packed median (s) | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 512  | 6.24   | 6.52   | 1.04 |
| 1024 | 24.84  | 26.92  | 1.08 |
| 2048 | 109.26 | 104.13 | 0.95 |
| 4096 | 618.82 | 630.35 | 1.02 |

Ratios span 0.95–1.08 with no consistent direction as n grows — a ±8% noise band on a
shared desktop GPU, not a trend. The n=2048 ×0.95 point (packed faster than stock) should
not be read as a cache-crossover effect without further evidence; it is within the same
noise band as the other three sizes.

Two caveats on comparability: the stock wall time includes the XQMX→array extraction that
the packed driver skips (host-side milliseconds against 6–618 s solves; the bias favours
packing and does not affect the verdict). And this neutrality is specific to the current
single-thread-per-block kernel — an occupancy-fixed kernel would change the memory-traffic
profile, so packing's throughput impact would need remeasuring there.

## 3. Faithfulness

Exact best-sample equality held between stock and packed at every size (512, 1024, 2048,
4096) — same seed, same beta schedule, bit-for-bit identical best sample vectors. (Stock's
`energy` is a host-recomputed integer; packed's `energy` is the kernel's raw float from a
different code path, so the two `energy` fields are not directly compared — the sample
equality is the faithfulness check.)

## 4. Metal analysis (no hardware)

`SolverMetalGPU` has no randoms buffer at all — acceptance randomness uses an on-device
xorshift32 RNG seeded per replica (`xqsa/metal_gpu.py` docstring), and `J` is float32
(`n²×4`) rather than CUDA's float64 (`n²×8`). Spins are stored dense `int32`, identically
to CUDA. Recomputing the same analytic breakdown with the randoms buffer removed and `J`
halved:

| n | J (float32, n²×4) | dense samples (int32) | spins' % of Metal total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 512  | 1.00 MiB   | 0.125 MiB | 11.09% |
| 1024 | 4.00 MiB   | 0.25 MiB  | 5.88%  |
| 2048 | 16.00 MiB  | 0.5 MiB   | 3.03%  |
| 4096 | 64.00 MiB  | 1.0 MiB   | 1.54%  |

(The percentage denominator also includes the float32 `h` bias buffer (n×4 B), not shown
as its own column — same convention as the CUDA table.)

With no randoms buffer to dilute the picture, spins' share of the Metal footprint is
visibly larger than CUDA's (<0.1%) at small n — but it falls monotonically as n grows,
because `J` scales as n² and the dense sample buffer scales as n. At the largest size
tested it is already down to 1.54%, and the gap only widens for n beyond 4096. The
conclusion carries over a fortiori: `J` is the dominant term on Metal too, and increasingly
so at the sizes the ticket cares about, so bit-packing spins would not be material there
either.

## 5. Recommendation: REJECT

Per the ticket's criterion ("material footprint reduction without unacceptable throughput
regression"):

- **Footprint:** spins are 0.089%–0.098% of the CUDA stock footprint across all four sizes
  measured, and packing would only shrink that already-negligible slice to 0.0028%–0.0031%.
  In absolute terms, packing saves at most 992 KiB (under 1 MiB) at n=4096, the largest size
  tested — not material against a >1 GiB total footprint dominated by the randoms buffer
  (88.6%–98.3%). The Metal analytic case (§4) is the more favorable one for adoption (no
  randoms buffer) and still shows spins falling to 1.54% of total by n=4096, trending
  toward zero as n grows further.
- **Throughput:** packed/stock ratios are flat within a ±8% noise band (0.95–1.08) across
  all four sizes, with no directional trend — packing neither reliably helps nor
  reliably hurts wall time at these sizes.

Adopting bit-packed spin storage would add kernel and host-side complexity (bit read/write
in the sweep loop, host-side pack/unpack) for a footprint saving of well under 1 MiB and no
measurable throughput change. **Reject** — the measured data does not support adoption.

## 6. Follow-up candidates (documented, not implemented)

1. **CUDA on-device RNG.** The randoms buffer is the actual dominant allocation on CUDA
   (88.6%–98.3% of footprint measured here) and the real blocker for large-n CUDA solves
   (the solver's 80%-of-VRAM guard). Replacing the pre-generated `(num_reads, num_sweeps,
   n)` float64 buffer with an on-device xorshift32 RNG — mirroring what
   `SolverMetalGPU` already does — would eliminate the dominant buffer outright, lift the
   80%-VRAM ceiling, and re-align the two solvers' RNG strategies. This is a materially
   larger win than spin-packing and worth its own ticket.
2. **CUDA kernel occupancy.** The stock kernel launches one *single-thread* block per
   replica (`(num_reads,), (1,)`), leaving the GPU almost entirely idle per solve. This
   bench's own n=4096 runs took ~618–670 s per solve (3 reps) on an RTX 4090 — visibly
   consistent with QUI-167's finding that CUDA solve is ~5x slower than
   `SolverDWaveCPU` at MaxCut 512. Fixing occupancy (one thread per spin, or per some
   reasonable tile, instead of one thread per replica) is a much larger and more certain
   throughput lever than anything spin-packing offers.

Both are proposed as candidate follow-up tickets, to be filed on Konrad's say-so.
